# 5-Tea Fertilizer Guide

> **Archived personal reference.** This guide was Alex’s personal organic fertilizer system and was removed from the shared CropManager app so that each farmer can develop and record their own approach. It is kept here as a historical reference, not as a universal recommendation. Adjust everything to your soil, crops, climate, and local extension advice.

## The five components

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| cow manure tea | Balanced N + organic matter + microbes. Gentle, safe for seedlings. |
| chicken manure tea | High N + P. Powerful vegetative booster. Use sparingly — can burn. |
| plant-based tea | NPK + micronutrients + mild pest suppression. Safe for all stages. |
| wood ash tea | K + Ca + Mg. Raises pH. Flowering/fruiting booster. Avoid for potatoes and leafy greens. |
| yeast ferment | Microbial stimulant. B-vitamins + root growth hormones. Keep doses small. |

## Yeast preparation

Dissolve 1 packet (10g) baker's yeast + 1-2 tbsp sugar in 1L lukewarm water (~30C). Ferment 12-24h until bubbling. Use within 24h of activation.

### Yeast dosing

- **foliar spray:** 1 tsp per 1 litre of final diluted mix
- **soil drench:** 1 tbsp per 5 litres of final diluted mix

## Mosquito control

Add 2-3 drops thyme oil per litre of stored tea to deter mosquito breeding. Add after brewing, just before application.

## How to read the dilutions

final_dilution is TEA CONCENTRATE parts : WATER parts. e.g. 25 means 1 cup tea per 25 cups water. mix_parts shows relative proportions of each tea concentrate to combine BEFORE final dilution with water.

## Application rules

- Foliar spray: early morning or late afternoon only — never midday sun
- Drench: apply at root zone, avoid stem contact
- Never mix teas with fungicides or pesticides — apply 3-5 days apart
- Never mix wood ash tea with chicken manure tea in the same application
- Stir teas daily to maintain aerobic conditions and prevent bad fermentation
- Use teas within 3-5 days of brewing for best microbial activity
- Always dilute before applying — never apply concentrate directly to plants
